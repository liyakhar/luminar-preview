How to read research papers faster

By the Luminar team · Updated October 15, 2026

Short answer: don't read a paper from start to end. Use three passes: first the title, abstract, figures and conclusion (5 to 10 minutes); then the introduction and results, skipping the math; and only if it matters, a full careful read. An AI summary can speed up the first pass, as long as you check its claims against the paper.

The three-pass method

This approach, described by S. Keshav in "How to Read a Paper", is still the simplest way to read more papers in less time.

Pass 1: is this paper for me? (5 to 10 minutes)

  • Read the title, abstract and conclusion.
  • Look at every figure and table, and read the captions.
  • Skim the section headings.

Now decide: read on, keep for later, or skip.

Pass 2: what does it claim? (about an hour)

  • Read the introduction and the results closely.
  • Skip proofs and heavy math for now.
  • Write down the question, the method, the main result and the limits in your own words.

Pass 3: could I redo it? (only when it matters)

Read everything, including the methods and the math, as if you had to repeat the work. Save this for the few papers at the center of your own work.

Read in this order, not front to back

  1. Abstract
  2. Figures and tables
  3. Conclusion and discussion
  4. Introduction
  5. Results
  6. Methods, only if you need them

Keep a reading log

For each paper, keep four lines: the question, the method, the result and the limits. After a month you will have your own review of the field, and you will not reread papers you already know.

Where AI summaries help, and where they don't

An AI summary is good for pass 1: it gives you the question, method, results and limits in plain words, so you can triage a stack of papers fast. It is not a replacement for pass 2 or 3, and you should never cite a summary instead of the paper.
