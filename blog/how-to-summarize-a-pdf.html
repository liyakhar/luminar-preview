How to summarize a PDF without missing what matters

By the Luminar team · Updated October 15, 2026

Short answer: read the title, abstract or executive summary, headings and conclusion first. Then write one sentence per section in your own words. For long files, an AI summarizer can do the first pass in minutes. Use one that cites where each point came from, and check the points you rely on.

1. Read the parts that summarize themselves

Most long PDFs already tell you what they say. Read these first, in this order:

  1. The title and the abstract or executive summary.
  2. The table of contents or the headings.
  3. The conclusion or recommendations.
  4. Tables and charts, with their captions.

After ten minutes you will know what the document claims and where the evidence is.

2. Write one sentence per section

Go section by section and write one sentence that says what it adds. Use your own words. If you cannot write the sentence, that section needs a closer read. The list of sentences is your summary.

3. Keep the numbers and the limits

Summaries often lose two things that matter most: the exact numbers and the caveats. Note key figures with their page number, and note anything the author says the work does not cover.

4. Let an AI do the first pass, then check it

For a 50-page report, an AI summarizer saves real time. The danger is a confident summary that gets a number or a claim wrong. Two habits fix most of that:

  • Use a tool that cites its sources, so every point points back to the text.
  • Check every point you plan to use against the original page.

A note on scanned PDFs

If the PDF is a scan, its pages are pictures and there may be no text to read. Try selecting a word in the file. If you cannot, run it through text recognition (OCR) first, or the summary will be empty or wrong.
